## Project Overview

This project is a short technical video analyzing the internal operation of a **hydrostatic transmission** using the **language and analytical framework of fluid mechanics**. Rather than presenting a mechanical teardown, the goal was to describe how the system operates through **governing equations, control-volume analysis, and well-defined modeling assumptions**, consistent with upper-level fluid mechanics coursework.

The transmission examined is representative of a small axial-piston hydrostatic unit. The analysis focuses on the interaction between the **pump rotating group**, **motor rotating group**, and internal leakage paths that determine speed, torque, and efficiency.



## My Role and Contributions

My primary contribution to this project was the **development and clarification of the governing fluid-mechanical equations and assumptions** used to analyze the transmission. Specifically, I worked on:

- Formulating the **pump flow relation** based on positive-displacement kinematics and swash-plate geometry.
- Explaining why the **motor speed and torque relations are independent of swash-plate angle**, due to the motor’s fixed displacement.
- Distinguishing between **pump-generated flow** and **motor-received flow**, and accounting for internal leakage.
- Defining and justifying the **modeling assumptions**, including incompressible flow, steady-periodic operation, rigid chambers, negligible kinetic-energy changes, and thin-film laminar leakage.
- Ensuring the equations were presented at a level where system performance could be **predicted quantitatively**, rather than described qualitatively.



## Key Concepts Addressed

- Positive-displacement pump kinematics  
- Continuity applied to rotating control volumes  
- Pressure–torque relationships from momentum balance  
- Hydraulic power from the steady-flow energy equation  
- Leakage modeling using lubrication theory  
- Role of assumptions in simplifying real fluid machines

## Takeaway

This project emphasizes the importance of **carefully chosen governing equations and assumptions** when analyzing real engineering systems. By framing the hydrostatic transmission through fluid-mechanics principles, the system’s behavior becomes analytically predictable and physically interpretable.
